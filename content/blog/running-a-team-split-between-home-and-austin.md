A founder at the IFN meetup describes their mornings since moving to Austin. They wake up to a full inbox from the team at home, and half the messages are waiting on a yes from them. By the time they have answered, the home team has logged off for the night. In the afternoon a customer emails a question only an engineer at home can answer, and it waits until tomorrow. The founder says the company feels slower since the move, even though everyone is working hard. Someone at the table says the same thing happened to them, and the fix turned out to be fairly boring.

Some version of this comes up most months. This post is peer orientation on running a team split between home and Austin: the overlap window, who the US-facing person is, handoffs that do not lose a day, and where decisions get made. It is about how the work runs day to day. It is not a management framework, and it does not cover the legal or tax side of where people work.

## Disclaimer

**This is not legal, tax, immigration, or financial advice.** IFN shares what founders in the room tend to talk through. Where team members can work from, how they are engaged, and what your own location means for the company all depend on your facts. Verify with licensed professionals and official sources before you act.

## Name the overlap window and protect it

Most split teams have some stretch of the day when both sides are awake and working. Founders say the first fix was simply to name it. Pick the hours, put them on everyone's calendar, and keep them for decisions and live conversation, not for updates that could have been written down.

Then someone has to pay for the window. If home is far ahead of Austin, either the founder takes early mornings or the home team takes late evenings. Peers who let one side carry it every day say it wore thin within a few months. Some rotate, with early calls for the founder some weeks and later calls for the team in others. Some split it by day of the week. The pattern matters less than agreeing on it out loud.

One small trap: clocks do not change for daylight saving on the same dates everywhere, and some countries do not change them at all. For a few weeks a year the window can shift by an hour without anyone noticing, until a meeting goes half empty. Founders who got caught once now check it twice a year.

## Decide who the US-facing person is

Customers, partners, and the people you meet in Austin need one name to contact. At first that is usually the founder, because they are the one here. The trouble is that the founder then becomes a relay. A customer asks a technical question, the founder forwards it home, waits overnight, sends the answer back, and every exchange gains a day.

Peers describe a point where they had to stop relaying. Some gave a senior person at home direct customer contact during the overlap window, with the founder copied rather than in the middle. Some made their first US hire the front door for day-to-day questions. Others kept the founder as the face but let the home team answer directly in writing. The [go-to-market post](/blog/us-go-to-market-when-your-product-was-built-elsewhere) covers why US buyers care about clear support hours, and the same logic holds inside the company. People can work with a team in another time zone. What frustrates them is not knowing who will answer, or when.

## Handoffs that do not stall for a day

The slow part of a split team is rarely the work itself. It is the moment one side finishes for the day and the other side picks up.

Founders who fixed this describe a habit more than a tool. At the end of the day, whoever is logging off writes a short handoff as if the reader cannot ask a single question until tomorrow, because they cannot. What got decided, what is blocked and why, who can unblock it, and what the other side can decide without waiting.

That last part is where most of the lost time hides. If the home team needs the founder's yes for every pricing exception, customer reply, or small product change, each of those waits a full cycle. Peers sit down once and agree on what the home team can decide alone, what needs a short written note, and what really needs the founder. Then they widen the first group as trust builds.

Some founders keep one short daily call in the overlap strictly for unblocking: no updates, only decisions that are stuck. Others find writing works better for their team. Either can work. Trouble starts when nobody owns the handoff.

## Where decisions actually get made

In a split team, decisions drift. Something gets decided on a call with whoever happened to be awake. Something else gets decided in a hallway in Austin. A week later, half the team is working from an old plan.

Peers describe two ways this goes wrong. In the first, the home team makes a call while the founder sleeps, and the founder reverses it in the morning, which teaches the team to stop deciding things. In the second, the founder agrees to something in Austin after a customer meeting and forgets to tell anyone at home until it has already been promised.

The fix founders mention is plain: one written place where decisions land, in whatever tool the team already uses, with a line on what was decided, who decided, and why. It does not need to be formal. It needs to be the place everyone checks before acting, the founder included.

## Keep the home team close

Something founders mention more quietly is how the move feels to the people at home. When the founder relocates, some of the team can feel left behind, or start to believe the interesting work now happens in Austin.

A few habits come up. Share what you learn from US customers and from the Austin room, not only requests. Bring people from home over when you can, even briefly, so the US side is real to them. Keep some of your attention on home-team problems even in a busy Austin week. And if you are building a local network here, as the [first 50 connections post](/blog/building-your-first-50-connections-in-austin) describes, let the team see what it brings back to the company.

## When we stop talking

The room shares how split teams run day to day. Some questions sit outside that and need licensed help:

- How people in each country are engaged or employed, and what changes if someone relocates: employment counsel in the relevant country
- What the founder's move, or where team members work from, means for company or personal tax: a cross-border CPA
- What your own status allows you to do while you are in the US: immigration counsel

Peers raise these as questions and stop there.
